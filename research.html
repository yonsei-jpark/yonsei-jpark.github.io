Spatial MIMO Channel Rendering with 3D Gaussian Splatting

 

  • Which transmit and receive beams will work well for a user at a location where the channel has never been measured? We learn how beam-pair power varies across a site from channel measurements at a sparse set of locations. Given a new user location, we predict a map whose entries give the long-term received power for each transmit and receive beam pair.

 

  • Our MIMO Gaussian splatting method (MIMO-GS) represents the propagation environment with pairs of 3D Gaussians. For each pair, we project one Gaussian onto the base station's transmit beam grid and the other onto the user's receive beam grid. Adding their weighted contributions across all pairs produces the power map. A base station can use these maps to select users and beams jointly while accounting for interference. In our multiuser evaluation, this scheduling approach achieved 98% of the genie-aided benchmark's net sum rate without sounding the candidate users.

Related Publications

  1. J. Han, G. Kim, C. Wen, A. Alkhateeb, J. Zhang, and J. Park, “Spatial MIMO Channel Rendering: A 3D Gaussian Splatting Approach,” submitted to IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications.

Reverse Mercury/Waterfilling for Fronthaul Compression

  • In an uplink cloud radio access network, each radio unit compresses its received signal before sending it to a central processor. With a fixed fronthaul budget, how many bits should each signal coefficient receive? Once a receiver can reliably distinguish all four symbols of a 4-QAM constellation, finer quantization adds little information. The same bits can be more valuable on a channel carrying a larger constellation.

 

  • Our reverse mercury/waterfilling (RMWF) rule accounts for this saturation. In the figure, a thick mercury layer marks the 4-QAM channel where extra precision yields little gain. RMWF shifts bits to the 256-QAM channel, even though its signal is weaker. With a shared 12-bit budget, the two channels achieve a sum generalized mutual information (GMI) of about 5.13 bits per symbol, compared with 4.00 for classical reverse waterfilling.

 

  • The table connects fronthaul compression to transmit power allocation. Mercury/waterfilling accounts for the modulation alphabet when allocating transmit power. RMWF applies the same principle to quantization bits, using the gain in GMI after linear MMSE detection to guide their allocation.

Attention-aided MMSE for OFDM Channel Estimation

 

  • Accurate channel estimation is essential for reliable OFDM communication, yet conventional MMSE methods rely on hard-to-obtain channel statistics, and deep learning models often suffer from high complexity and low interpretability.

  • To address this, we propose Attention-aided MMSE (A-MMSE), a model-based learning approach that leverages an Attention Transformer to learn a linear estimation filter. Once trained, the filter enables fast and efficient channel estimation using only linear operations. A two-stage attention encoder explicitly captures frequency and temporal correlations in the channel, leading to significantly improved accuracy and robustness. A rank-adaptive extension allows flexible control over complexity, making it suitable for real-world 5G and upcoming 6G systems.

Massive MIMO for Multibeam Satellites

 

  • Massive MIMO is a key enabler for next-generation satellite networks, offering high spectral efficiency and spatial multiplexing gains over wide coverage areas. Yet satellite channels differ fundamentally from terrestrial ones: they are dominated by line-of-sight (LoS) propagation, shaped by 3D geometry with uniform planar arrays (UPAs), and subject to large Doppler shifts arising from fast satellite motion. These features call for a rethinking of precoding, user scheduling, and beamforming principles that were originally developed for rich-scattering terrestrial systems.

  • Our group studies massive MIMO for satellite communications from both an analytical and an algorithmic perspective. On the analysis side, we characterize the asymptotic rate behavior of multibeam and multicast systems, revisiting multi-user diversity under LoS channels and 3D array geometry, and deriving how user density must scale with the number of antennas and beams to preserve asymptotic optimality. On the design side, we develop space-time beamforming methods that exploit the Doppler dimension as an additional signaling resource, enabling extremely narrow beams and robust transmission under spatial user crowding.

Space-Time Adaptive Beamforming (STAB) for LEO Satellites

 

  • Two users a few streets apart can lie in nearly the same direction when viewed from a low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite. Their line-of-sight channels then look similar across the antenna array, making it difficult to transmit separate data streams to them on the same time and frequency resources.

 

  • We model this crowding by dividing the angular field into bins set by the array's resolution and counting the users in the busiest bin. This lets us identify a user-density threshold beyond which the sum rate of spatial zero-forcing precoding vanishes as the system scales.

 

  • Space-time adaptive beamforming (STAB) uses the fact that users at similar angles can have different residual Doppler shifts. Their channel phases then evolve at different rates. By stacking the antenna channels across successive time slots, STAB can separate users that the array alone struggles to resolve. Our space-Doppler scheduler uses both angle and Doppler when selecting users to serve together.

Shot in the Dark – MIMO without CSIT

 

  • In MIMO downlink systems, the acquisition of accurate CSIT has long been a major challenge, especially in scenarios with limited feedback or rapidly changing channels.

  • In our recent work, we propose a novel framework that avoids reliance on explicit CSIT feedback by leveraging partial channel reciprocity between the uplink and downlink. By exploiting shared channel characteristics, the proposed method directly predicts the downlink CSI from the uplink estimated CSI, enabling efficient and robust downlink transmission with significantly reduced overhead associated with CSI.

Y-Twin (made with Sionna)

 

  • We have developed a custom digital twin, named Y-Twin, which fully reflects the geometry, traffic dynamics, and mobility patterns of the Sinchon area near Yonsei University. Built on top of the Sionna framework, Y-Twin supports fully functioning RAN emulation, enabling end-to-end wireless system evaluation in a realistic urban environment. This platform is expected to serve as a foundation for a wide range of AI-native RAN research in the near future.

  • Dataset, structure, and usage details will be made available soon.

State Estimation with 1-bit Observation and Imperfect Model

 

  • State estimation is an important problem for estimating a system’s hidden state, and Kalman filter is commonly used for this purpose. Most of the existing algorithms assume ideal environment (infinite resolution or full model knowledge, etc), so their performance degrades in real-world environments.

  • This work considers a state estimation problem with 1-bit observation and imperfect models. By using the well-knonw Bussgang theorem to approximate nonlinear inputs linearly, we get a linear filter structure while accounting for ADC quantization distortions. To overcome model uncertainties and nonlinearities, we propose a hybrid DNN approach that integrates Bussgang-based linearization with data-driven learning for robust and efficient inference.

MIMO-ME-MS Channel

 

  • In this work, we introduce the MIMO-ME-MS channel, which comprises a multi-antenna transmitter and receiver, along with a multi-antenna eavesdropper and sensing receiver. Within this framework, we investigate the quasi-optimal precoder structure via subspace analysis and propose an iterative precoding design method tailored to this setting.
